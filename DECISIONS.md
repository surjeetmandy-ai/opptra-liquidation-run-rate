# Decisions

The judgement calls behind the tool, and why. Where the build changed my mind, it says so.

| # | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The objective is net recovery in AED**, measured against jobbering everything today (AED 83k on this stock). Sell-through is shown, not optimised. | Sell-through can be bought. A full clear at the floor with AED 300 of ads per pair is worse than jobbering those pairs now. |
| 2 | **The jobber is a daily option**, with partial exits. | The brief says any day, any quantity. S19 (260 pairs, 3 sales in 14 days, ~AED 260/day of ads) shows what treating it as a day-60 event costs. |
| 3 | **Hold is the default**; a change has to beat it by a margin (AED 300 for a price move, 150 for ads). | An unneeded cut gives away margin on pairs that would have sold anyway, and price slots are scarce. 14 listings are already on track. |
| 4 | **The LLM is outside the decision loop.** It writes the brief and flags; it never produces a number that gets applied. | One broken rule is a failure, and I can test code against a rolling-window count but not a prompt. Numbers in the brief are checked against the plan, and any that aren't found are shown. |
| 5 | **Diagnose before choosing a lever.** Thin traffic with good conversion → ads. Traffic fine, conversion low → price. Both → price first, ads only where they pay. Near-zero conversion at a deep discount → stop spending, flag for a human. | Buying traffic for a listing that doesn't convert is the expensive mistake, and a listing nobody buys at 65% off has a problem no price fixes. |
| 6 | **Conversion is shrunk toward category × channel peers** (Beta-Binomial, prior worth 150 sessions; last 7 days weighted double). | 14 days of ~60 sessions a day is thin. A listing with 40 sessions borrows from its peers; one with 400 speaks for itself. |
| 7 | **Elasticity is one prior (2.0 ± 0.6), updated only from observed price changes.** | The history has one price change, and it is a parity move. Per-category elasticities would be false precision. The two-changes-a-week limit doubles as the experiment budget. |
| 8 | **Noon sessions on 29–30 Sep are treated as missing; units are trusted.** | Sessions below ad clicks is impossible. Read naively, Noon's conversion goes to infinity and every Noon listing looks like it deserves ads. |
| 9 | **Reallocate ads before adding them; every raise has to project a gain (AED 150) at the planned price.** | Budgets were ~90% spent; the money was in the wrong places. Amazon.ae goes from AED 1,342 to ~465/day on day 1: most of it was buying sales that paid less than the jobber or sales that would have happened anyway, and the biggest raises that survive (S03 Amazon.ae, S14 Noon) are on listings converting at ~3% on ~20 sessions a day, where a dirham of traffic has the most to work on. Each AED 10 is valued with the same projection the plan is judged by, so a raise can't look good in the allocator and bad in the verdict. |
| 10 | **Cut sizing: the smallest step that closes the gap; else, for a critical style, the legal cut with the best projected recovery (within the 20% step); for a behind one, a measured ~10% first move; then 3 days before cutting again.** | *Changed in the build.* My first version maximised projected net recovery for every listing, and with elasticity above 1 that drove cuts to the 20% bound even for listings only slightly behind: the bound was deciding, not the economics. Maximising is now reserved for styles that will not make it otherwise. |
| 11 | **Prices are chosen per style, as a pair.** The follower channel is labelled, and the style-level number is shown next to both. | Parity makes Noon move when Amazon moves, and that costs Noon a slot. Sometimes a cut that pays on one channel loses on the style; the tool holds and says why (S12). |
| 12 | **Exits use the optimistic read** (90th-percentile conversion, elasticity +1 SD): only units that won't sell even then. | An exit can't be undone; a later exit is always possible. Broken size runs (S04: 60% of stock in 39/45/46) are where it bites first. |
| 13 | **One move per listing per day; a 3-day cooldown on both levers.** | One day of data after a change is noise; reacting to it is how tools churn. |
| 14 | **The tool checks its own forecasts.** Each applied action logs a predicted run-rate; once 4+ actions have ≥3 days of actuals, each lever's *uplift* is re-weighted by Σ(actual − baseline) / Σ(predicted − baseline), bounded 0.5–1.5, and applied to the uplift only, never the baseline. | With this little data, that is the honest form of "gets better over time". Measuring raw actual/predicted was wrong: a good listing passes that test whatever the lever did. In an 8-day rehearsal on simulated days the re-weighting started on day 4 and moved with each day's data; with a handful of scored actions it is noisy, which is why it is bounded. |
| 15 | **Rules already broken in incoming data are fixed first; unfixable ones are shown as conflicts.** | *Added in the build.* Stress-testing a raised floor and a tighter parity band showed the tool otherwise couldn't produce a legal plan at all. |
| 16 | **Marketplace fees default to 0**, as a slider. | The brief has none; a guessed fee would move every number without evidence. In production it goes in on day one. |
| 17 | **Day 1 is big (28 actions), later days small (2–19 in an 8-day rehearsal, most days under 10; day 4 is the second big one, when the checked listings' exits fall due).** The page shows the top 8 and an Apply-all. | Day 1 fixes the leaks. If it proposed 28 actions on day 20, something would be wrong. |
| 18 | **Pydantic where data comes in from outside; dataclasses for what the code builds itself.** Settings (env/.env), the LLM's reply, `state.json` and the sidebar overrides are validated; `Action`, `Plan`, `Projection` are dataclasses. | Validation belongs at the boundary, where input can be wrong. Inside, it only costs time: the price search builds thousands of projections per plan. A damaged `state.json` stops the app with a clear message instead of starting fresh, because forgetting applied price changes could break the two-changes-a-week rule. |
| 19 | **Constants live with the code that gives them meaning; shared ones get one home named for what they are.** No catch-all `constants.py`. The input contract (required/optional/numeric columns per file) and the channel names are in `engine/schema.py`; everything else uses column names directly, as pandas code does. | The contract is checked once, at upload, so a missing column says "inventory is missing columns: units_on_hand" instead of a `KeyError` deep in the estimates. Channel ids were typed out 19 times, including in the parity rule; one typo there would silently stop the rule applying. The same rule applies to classes: each lives with the code that builds it (`Dataset` with the loader, `Plan` with `decide`), except `Action`, which every part of the tool uses and nothing owns, so it has its own `actions.py`. |
| 20 | **Every public module, class and function has a docstring, enforced by ruff (D1) in CI.** Docstrings say what a function does and anything non-obvious (units, side effects, why); type hints cover the arguments, so no Args/Returns boilerplate. | Leaving out the "obvious" ones was a function-by-function judgement, and it left 58 gaps; a reader can't tell "obvious" from "forgotten". A rule the linter checks doesn't depend on anyone's judgement. |
| 21 | **Planning is pure; side effects live in one place.** `build_plan` reads data and state and returns a plan; replaying exits, scoring predictions, fixing day 1 and keeping uploads under `.session/` happen in `engine/session.py`, which the page, `run_plan.py` and the drills all go through. Stopping ads is one rule (`should_stop_ads`), judged by the projection. | *From the review.* Three entry points had drifted: the terminal planned from the original inventory while the page had rolled stock forward, and a refresh lost the uploaded day. A stop decided by per-sale cost once contradicted the impact shown next to it (S18); one rule, one projection, no contradiction. |
| 22 | **A broken listing is checked before it is exited; other exits are read at the price the listing is heading for.** A `check` action records the date; the exit comes 3 days later if the listing is still dead. | *From the second review.* The first build exited 422 pairs on day 1, 234 of them from S19, which it was simultaneously flagging as probably broken (buy box, images). Exits are irreversible and pay a quarter of cost; a fixed listing recovers three to four times that, and three days of holding cost AED 70. Day 1 now exits 79 pairs. |
| 23 | **Ads are set to the spend the projection values highest between zero and today's, not switched off wholesale.** Stop if zero is best, trim otherwise; a raise must project +AED 150. | *From the second review.* The binary stop turned S01 Amazon.ae (on track at 1.9x) off entirely when AED 70/day was worth AED 1,100 more than zero by the same projection, and the card's reason ("each sale costs more than the jobber margin") was false for 10 of 17 stops. One search, one reason chosen by which case is true. |
| 24 | **Three test layers, and a map from every rule to its test** (`VERIFICATION.md`). The brief re-stated as a checker that imports nothing from the engine's guardrail; each rule at its exact boundary on hand-built fixtures; the engine on the real data. | An independent mutation audit found that half of 51 realistic bugs (a 20x allocator cap removed, a zero price gate, a 6-day window, swapped budget caps) survived the original 55 tests, most of which asserted "no violations", which the guardrail guarantees by construction. Legal is not the same as right; the new tests pin the decisions, not just the rules. |
| 25 | **The brief is Claude Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort, through the official `anthropic` SDK, with the reply's shape enforced by structured outputs.** One provider. | Earlier versions called three providers over plain HTTPS. One provider through its SDK is less code and gets retries, timeouts and typed errors from the library instead of hand-rolled ones; every SDK error maps to a sentence telling Farah what to do. Sonnet over Haiku: the brief is the one place where judgement in plain language matters (which anomaly is worth a human look), at ~3 US cents a refresh. Over Opus or Fable: no deep reasoning is needed to explain a plan that is already computed. Medium effort because Sonnet 5.5 thinks by default and the page is waiting. The schema is generated from the same pydantic class that validates the reply, so they can't drift. `LLM_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5` is the cheap, fast fallback. |
